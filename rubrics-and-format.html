Speech Grading Rubric

Final report · Out of 100 points

Used to evaluate all video speech submissions across the Primary, Middle, and High School divisions. Categories are weighted to reward substantive engagement above presentation polish, with technical quality treated as a smaller modifier.

Category Max Exceptional Proficient Developing Beginning
Research & Critical Thinking 30 Top
Insightful and original ideas; strong use of diverse, credible sources; excellent critical thinking and synthesis.		 Mid-upper
Clear, supported ideas; good research and reasonable analysis.		 Mid
Some evidence of research; limited depth or analysis.		 Lower
Minimal or unclear research; weak or unsupported ideas.
Organization & Structure 30 Top
Well-organized, logical flow; strong intro and conclusion; smooth transitions throughout.		 Mid-upper
Clear structure with minor flow issues; intro and conclusion are present.		 Mid
Some organization, but transitions are rough or flow is uneven.		 Lower
Disorganized or hard to follow; lacks clear structure.
Delivery & Presentation 30 Top
Confident, engaging, and polished delivery; strong eye contact, tone, pacing, and presence.		 Mid-upper
Generally clear and confident; minor delivery flaws; good engagement.		 Mid
Inconsistent or flat delivery; some clarity or engagement issues.		 Lower
Monotone, unclear, or unconfident; poor audience engagement.
Technical Quality 10 Top
High-quality video and audio; professional framing, lighting, and sound throughout.		 Mid-upper
Generally good technical quality with minor issues.		 Mid
Some distracting issues with sound, lighting, or framing.		 Lower
Major technical problems; poor video/audio significantly disrupts delivery.

Essay Grading Rubric

Progress report · Out of 100 points · 17–20 Exceptional · 13–16 Proficient · 9–12 Developing · 0–8 Beginning

Used to evaluate the 1,500-word team essays in the Essay & Debate track. Five equally weighted categories of 20 points each. Strong essays carry their authors into the live debate elimination rounds.

Category Max Exceptional Proficient Developing Beginning
Content & Argumentation 20 17–20
Sophisticated, focused thesis; strong line of reasoning; ideas developed with clarity and insight.		 13–16
Clear and relevant thesis; arguments mostly logical and supported.		 9–12
Basic or partially developed thesis; some relevant ideas but lacks depth.		 0–8
Unclear or weak thesis; minimal argument development.
Evidence Integration 20 17–20
Diverse, credible sources used seamlessly; clear and thoughtful analysis of evidence.		 13–16
Reliable sources; generally well-integrated with some explanation.		 9–12
Limited or somewhat relevant sources; basic or uneven integration.		 0–8
Few or no credible sources; little to no integration or analysis.
Reasoning 20 17–20
Highly logical and coherent; demonstrates critical thinking throughout.		 13–16
Mostly logical; some original analysis or insight.		 9–12
Basic logical flow; limited or unclear analysis.		 0–8
Illogical, inconsistent, or unsupported reasoning.
Counter-Arguments 20 17–20
Effectively addresses and refutes opposing views; adds depth to argument.		 13–16
Acknowledges opposing views; basic rebuttals attempted.		 9–12

Section 03

Online Debate Format

Live elimination rounds · Essay & Debate track

The format used for teams who advance from the qualifying essay round into the live elimination bracket. Each round is structured to balance prepared argument, real-time engagement, and judge scrutiny.

Phase Affirmative Negative
Constructive Speech 5 minutes 5 minutes
First Crossfire 3 minutes — both teams
Rebuttal Speech 5 minutes 5 minutes
Second Crossfire 3 minutes — both teams
Summary Speech 3 minutes 3 minutes
Judges' Questions 7 minutes — alternating between teams
  • Constructive SpeechesEach side presents its main arguments to establish the foundation of the debate.
  • CrossfireBoth teams interact directly, asking questions and responding in real time to challenge or clarify points.
  • Rebuttal SpeechesTeams respond to their opponent's key arguments with focused counterpoints.
  • Summary SpeechesEach side refines and emphasizes its core arguments to wrap up the case.
  • Judges' QuestionsJudges follow up with each team, pressing for clarity and deeper justification.
